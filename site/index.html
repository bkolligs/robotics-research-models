Mention method and corpus coverage

Loading coverage…

A mention is a tracked model name found in the PDF text, including the bibliography. Each paper counts once per model. One paper can name several models, so stacked percentages can exceed 100%. A mention does not establish model use.

Counting method and dates

We scan cs.RO PDFs with pdftotext and match curated model aliases, keeping releases separate. Monthly shares divide paper counts by all scanned papers or papers naming a tracked model, as selected. Families, institutions, and countries each count a paper once per group. Country means the headquarters country of the organization that released the model. For example, a paper mentioning π0.5 counts once toward the United States because Physical Intelligence is based there, regardless of the paper authors’ affiliations. Failed extractions are excluded; — means no eligible papers.

No release-date gate is applied. Later revisions can mention models released after a paper’s first submission.

    Evaluation method and outcome definitions

    Jev classified each mentioned release from the title, abstract, and up to 60,000 characters of PDF excerpts around model names and experiments. Inclusion requires at least 70% probability for both baseline use and the authors’ own runs. Copied scores, citations, and component-only use are excluded. Recorded source reviews take precedence.

    Outcomes compare the proposed method with the baseline: a win, loss, or tie. Mixed results means evidence shows wins and losses across tasks or metrics, or a performance–efficiency tradeoff; there is no overall winner. Outcome unclear means comparator use is confirmed, but the winner is uncertain, results are missing, a table is ambiguous, or outcome confidence is below 70%. Evaluation only means there is no proposed method to compare.

    Each paper counts once per evaluated model. Outcome percentages use that model’s evaluation-paper count. Dates use first submission; excerpts come from the latest scanned revision.

    Lifecycle method and release sources

    The “Strongest baseline” score sums monthly confirmed post-release evaluation-paper counts over the observed corpus: score = Σ monthly papers. This is the area under the unsmoothed evaluations-per-month curve, with one-month bins. Each paper counts once per model. The introducing paper and pre-release comparisons are excluded; only models with verified release dates qualify. Missing months add no observations. The score measures accumulated baseline use, not wins or performance, and depends on corpus coverage and time since release.

    *Peaks require a full three-month window and at least 20 papers evaluating a tracked model. Shares can overlap. Publication dates lag experiments; a falling share does not by itself establish that a model has been superseded.

    Age starts at the sourced announcement or original paper, which can precede usable checkpoints. For π0, announcement was October 31, 2024; code and weights followed on February 4, 2025. The release month and first smoothing windows may cover only part of a month or fewer than three months. Earlier paper revisions can differ from the full text we scanned.

    Architecture survey method

    Jev classifies substantive topics from titles and abstracts of papers mentioning a tracked robotics model. Each trend is assessed independently. Monthly shares use all surveyed papers or papers positive for at least one trend, as selected. These judgments do not establish which model a paper used.

    Frontier LLM mentions and usage

    LLM mention and usage method

    Jev receives the title, abstract, and up to eight sampled passages around each model name. Usage requires at least 50% probability that the authors ran, integrated, or directly evaluated it. Copied results, background citations, future work, and writing assistance are excluded. Each paper counts once per release; both rates divide by all successfully scanned papers that month.
